## Our Heat Pump Suitability Map

Replacing a fossil fuel boiler with a low carbon heating technology, like a heat pump, is the best thing that most people can do to decarbonise their home. Low carbon heating technologies are suitable for most homes, however particular technologies may work better in some homes than others. 

This map shows the suitability of neighbourhoods across Great Britain for different low carbon heating teachnologies. The four technologies mapped are:
* **Air source heat pumps** - A hydronic (air-to-water) air source heat pump (ASHP) is powered by electricity and provides hot water to a cylinder and heat to radiators or underfloor heating by taking energy from the air outside, concentrating it and moving it into a house. Many homes will be suitable for an ASHP.
* **Ground source heat pumps** - A hydronic (air-to-water) ground source heat pump (GSHP) is powered by electricity and provides hot water to a cylinder and heat to radiators or underfloor heating by taking energy from the ground outside, concentrating it and moving it into a house. GSHPs tend to need more outside space than ASHP, cost more, but can be more efficient.
* **Shared ground loops** - A special kind of shared ground source heat pump, in which a large 'ground loop' provides enough energy from the ground that several homes can connect to it. Each connected home has their own heat pump powered by electricity which uses the shared ground loop. Most suitable for denser housing such as terraced streets or new build estates.
* **Heat networks** - A large central heat source, such as a heat pump, circulates heat through insulated pipes to connected homes in a neighbourhood or apartment building. Homes have a 'heat interface unit' which tracks consumption. Most suitable in very dense settings where other low carbon heating technologies are less viable.

While not shown on this map, air-to-air heat pumps (also known as reversible air conditioners) may also be a good option for some homes, especially flats and apartments.

### How to use this map

You can **select** the map layer you want to view using the drop box at the top of the page.

You can **pan** the map and **zoom** to see local patterns of suitability.

Once you have zoomed in enough to see the neighbourhoods highlighted, you can **click** on an neighbourhood to see more information about it.

You can **adjust the transparency** of the map with the slider at the top right of the page.

### Reading the map

Each neighbourhood has a score between 0 and 1 for each of the four technologies. Higher scores indicate higher suitability.

Some neighbourhoods are best suited to individual technologies like ASHP or GSHP. Homeowners or landlords will have to decide the best heat pump technology for them, but we expect many will adopt air source heat pumps.

Some neighourhoods are best suited to shared technologies like heat networks. They may be given the opportunity to join a scheme in the future.

The most interesting neighbourhoods may be suitable for both individual and shared technologies, for instance a terrace house might have sufficient outside space for an individual air source heat pump, but also be on a street that is a good prospect for a shared ground loop. Local energy planning may be able to give these homeowners and landlords clearer guidance on the best solution for their local area.
